About this validator

Freedom Yield is a small, transparent Metal Blockchain validator focused on public operational evidence rather than inflated capacity claims.

A Japan-based operator that has been participating in the Metallicus ecosystem.

Operating principle

On Snowman consensus, server specs are pass/fail — running larger machines does not earn more rewards. We operate at the official minimum and put the effort that would otherwise go into hardware into uptime, monitoring, and incident response:

  • Push-based anomaly detection (container status, disk, memory, peer count, validator drop)
  • Renewal-deadline alerts at T−7 days, T−1 day, T−0 day, and T−10 minutes (only the last carries urgent priority)
  • Daily status digest pushed to the operator at a fixed multi-time cadence
  • Disaster-recovery procedure verified by quarterly drills (encrypted backup, NodeID reproducibility)

Self-stake is intentionally small — currently on the lower end of the network. We deliberately do not chase yield by scaling our own commitment; capacity grows incrementally each renewal cycle. The live amount is in the status card above, served from /api/validator.json.

The raw building blocks of operations are published openly — full peer-set snapshots, validator-concentration history, fee-market percentiles, incident timelines, the renewal calendar. The catalog lives at /data/.

Infrastructure

  • Production-grade VPS comfortably above the official Metallicus minimum recommendation
  • NVMe-class local storage for chain data (no network filesystems on the validator's hot path)
  • Port 9651/TCP exposed for staking, all other inbound restricted
  • Independent host from this public website (failure isolation)

Note: validator-to-end-user latency does not affect delegators. Region choice is about peer RTT and operator SSH, not your experience.

Why delegate to us

  • 3% delegation fee

    One tier above the 2% protocol floor. Predictable take rate, no surprise hikes.

  • Active monitoring

    24/7 uptime probes, automated push alerts for container / disk / peer count anomalies, period-renewal guardrails.

  • Long-horizon participation

    The focus is on continuity over many cycles, not on short-term scaling.

  • No slashing on the network

    Metal Blockchain has no slashing. The downside of a downtime event is missed rewards — your principal is never at protocol risk.

  • Transparent track record

    Every metric on this page is fetched live from the validator. The status JSON is open at /api/validator.json.

One node among many

Metal Blockchain runs on a worldwide cooperative of validators — at the time of writing, ~200 independent operators take turns voting on every block. Our node is one of them, no more, no less. Snowman consensus rewards diversity, not dominance: the network is more resilient because of every other operator, not in spite of them.

Formula & payment rules

Reward = principal × APY × (lock days / 365) × uptime factor × (1 − 3% fee). Below 80% uptime the network pays no reward at all, so the 80% line marks the worst meaningful case.

Minimum requirements (network-wide)

  • Minimum delegation: 25 METAL
  • Lock period: 2 weeks to 1 year
  • No slashing — penalty for downtime is reward loss only
  • Funds lock for the chosen duration; rewards are released at period end

What's next

Roadmap

A validator is a long-horizon discipline, not a project with a finish line. Here's what we've committed to, and how we decide what comes next.

  1. 2026-05 → 2027-05 In progress

    Phase 1 — Accumulation

    Twelve monthly cycles. Each cycle: file the tx, run for ~30 days, renew. Self-stake grows incrementally as external capital is deployed (~3,000 METAL per cycle). Audit traces — uptime ledger, incidents, cadence — are published every cycle. Delegations are not actively pursued; the focus is operational discipline and record-keeping. Whether or not delegations arrive, the year produces what matters most: twelve cycle completions and a public track record.

  2. 2027-05 Decision point

    Phase 2 review

    Twelve cycle completions, twelve months of audit trace. At this point we reassess: extend cycle duration? Begin to actively accept delegations? The judgment is made from a year of evidence, not from a line drawn today.

  3. Open-ended Long-term

    Beyond Phase 2

    Continue contributing within the Metal Blockchain ecosystem as it evolves. Validator operation has no fixed finish line; the only durable commitment is to operational quality and to staying useful as the network changes around it.

FAQ

01Operations What happens if the validator goes offline?

If our node falls below 80% network-observed uptime over a period, the entire period's reward (for both the validator and its delegators) is forfeited. There is no slashing of principal — your delegated METAL is always returned when the lock period ends, regardless of validator behavior.

02Economics How long is the lock period?

You choose: anywhere from 14 days to 1 year. Longer periods earn slightly higher APY. Your delegation period cannot exceed the validator's remaining period.

03Economics When are rewards paid?

At the end of your chosen lock period, principal and rewards are released together to your reward address on the P-Chain. There are no intermediate payouts.

04Economics Can I withdraw my delegation early?

No. Metal Blockchain delegations are non-revocable. The duration you select at delegation time is final.

05Economics How is the 3% fee compared to other validators?

2% is the protocol floor; most active validators sit at 2–10%. We chose 3% — one tier above the floor — pricing the operational effort honestly.

06Operations What if your validator's period ends mid-delegation?

The protocol forbids accepting a delegation that would outlast the validator. You'll only be able to delegate up to the validator's remaining period. We re-register on time, so this is a soft constraint, not a real risk.

07Trust Is your code open?

Yes. This entire site, the monitoring scripts, the disaster-recovery procedure, and the validator's renewal runbook all live in a public Git repository so you can audit how the operation is run.
