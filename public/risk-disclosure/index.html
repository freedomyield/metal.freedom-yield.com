What a reasonable evaluator should surface

Risk disclosure

This page documents the known limitations and risks of delegating to or relying on this validator. The intent is honest disclosure, not deterrence. Each item is a fact a reasonable evaluator would surface during due diligence — we surface it ourselves.

Known risks

1. Small self-stake

Self-stake is small. Delegation capacity is limited to 5× self-stake by protocol design. Capacity grows only as the operator adds self-stake at renewal time. The current self-stake figure is published on the home status section and in the cycle journal; it does not change mid-cycle.

2. Single-region validator infrastructure

The validator runs on a single VPS in a single region. There is no multi-region failover. A regional outage of that provider results in validator downtime. The continuity page documents the recovery model from this scenario; it is recovery, not avoidance.

3. Delegation concentration

Few large delegators can concentrate received stake. A single large delegator leaving at cycle end materially changes the validator's economics. This is a fact of being a small validator and is not mitigated by any operator action available within the current scale.

4. No guaranteed subnet acceptance

This validator participates in Metal Blockchain's primary network. Inclusion in any future subnet validator set is at the discretion of the subnet operator. Nothing on this site implies pre-selection, allocation, or any bilateral arrangement with a subnet operator.

5. Public web availability is not validator uptime

The public site at metal.freedom-yield.com can be unreachable while metalgo keeps producing consensus signals. Conversely, the site can be up while the validator has degraded. Always verify validator state on chain via the explorer or /api/validator.json.

6. Subnet-specific requirements may require separate infrastructure

Validating a subnet typically requires additional metalgo process(es) and dedicated capacity. This validator's current infrastructure does not automatically cover subnet validation; that would be a separate undertaking, announced in advance per commitment §8 if and when undertaken.

7. Regulatory / compliance engagements require separate review

This validator is a primary-network participant. Regulated counterparties (banks, regulated funds, similar regulated entities) needing compliance attestation must perform independent bilateral due diligence; this page is not a compliance attestation, and nothing on this site should be read as one.

8. Single-operator dependency

This validator is operated by a single individual. There is no segregation of duties, no on-call rotation team, no independent change-approval board, and no organisational redundancy in operational decisions. The Constitution and Operating Model define an "AI proposes, operator approves, operator executes, AI verifies" pattern, but the final-approval and final-execution authorities are the same person. Response time outside Japan business hours is best-effort, not guaranteed. Operator unavailability at renewal time means the validator's current cycle ends and delegated stake is returned automatically (per the no-slashing property documented on the continuity page); the validator itself does not pose loss-of-principal risk from operator absence, but evaluators relying on continuous operator response should plan accordingly.
