What we hold ourselves to

Operator commitments

Nine explicit commitments — written so that breaking any of them is observable from the outside. These are not aspirational marketing statements; each is a behaviour a third party can audit by reading this site, the on-chain record, and the public repository.

The ten commitments

1. Renewal transparency

Every monthly cycle's AddPermissionlessValidatorTx ID is published with an explorer link on the cycle journal. Each entry includes period start / end, self-stake amount, fee, and the canonical explorer URL — so anyone can reconstruct the full renewal history from the public site without trusting any number rendered here.

2. Public incident disclosure

Every SEV-1 / SEV-2 incident is documented on the incidents page with timeline, root cause, and impact within 24 hours of resolution. The underlying JSON at /api/incidents.json is the source of truth — an empty list means an empty list, not a curated one.

3. Non-custodial operation

Delegations remain in delegators' wallets at all times. The operator can never withdraw, transfer, or freeze delegated METAL. The Avalanche-derived P-Chain protocol returns delegated stake to its owner at cycle end regardless of operator action or inaction.

4. No inflated infrastructure claims

Production-grade VPS that meets the Metallicus minimum recommendation. No "institutional-ready", "multi-region", "enterprise SLA", or "subnet operator" claims that exceed delivered reality. Current capacity is documented in continuity and risk disclosure.

5. Validator health first

When observability, UX polish, or new features would compete with metalgo health for resources, the validator process wins. Documented in docs/CONSTITUTION.md §2. In practice this means non-essential dashboards are degraded or paused before metalgo is ever constrained.

6. Web / validator separation

The validator host and the public web host are independent. A web outage does not affect metalgo uptime; a validator outage does not break the public site. Each side can be observed and recovered without touching the other — see continuity for the full scenario walkthrough.

7. No retail campaign focus

No SEO push, no growth content, no paid acquisition. The audience is institutional readers performing due diligence, not retail delegators chasing yield. This page being noindex is consistent with that — search engines are not the distribution channel.

8. Reasonable decommission notice policy

If this operator stops running the validator, a public notice is committed with at least 90 days advance lead time so delegators can rebalance. The notice appears on this site and as a commit in the public repository, both of which are timestamped and externally observable.

9. Security-sensitive information is not publicly exposed

Host paths, IP addresses, SSH credentials, BasicAuth hashes, ntfy topics, and similar are classified SECRET under docs/CONSTITUTION.md §4.1 and never appear in the public repository, site content, issues, or commit messages. Public surfaces show validator state, not the infrastructure that produces it.

10. 24-hour inquiry response target on business days

Incoming inquiries to info@metal.freedom-yield.com receive a first response within 24 hours on Japan business days (excluding weekends and Japanese national holidays). The first response either substantively answers the question or sets a clear bilateral next step; a single line acknowledging receipt is not the target. The inquiry surface and the structured information requested are documented on the inquiry page — no name or organisation is required to send one.

Reference

The constitutional document these commitments derive from is published at docs/CONSTITUTION.md in the public repository. The companion to this page — what the operator does not claim and the limits a reasonable evaluator should know — is the risk disclosure.
